01 / MOLECULAR MACHINE LEARNING

LeadOptAI

Master’s thesis research · Research prototype
My role: model development, training & evaluation

A chemistry-aware Transformer that connects molecular structure with continuous molecular properties. I developed LeadOptAI in Python and PyTorch for molecular property prediction and property-conditioned SMILES infilling, combining custom model design with RDKit data processing, training, and evaluation.

Research question
How can chemistry-aware masking and continuous property conditioning support molecular representation learning?
Delivered
A custom PyTorch Transformer with RDKit preprocessing, chemistry-aware masking, property prediction and SMILES infilling; training and evaluation workflows.
Scope
Research model and computational evaluation. Molecular descriptors provide context; they do not establish experimental ADMET performance.
PyTorchTransformersRDKitSMILES
Model architecture

Custom embeddings bring discrete SMILES tokens and continuous molecular properties into a shared representation.

Chemistry-aware learning

Functional-group and BRICS masking introduce chemically meaningful regions into the training process.

Training & evaluation

Molecular preprocessing, model training, benchmarking, hyperparameter optimisation, and ablation studies.

What does property-conditioned infilling mean?

The model uses supplied molecular properties as context when predicting masked parts of a SMILES string. This connects molecular reconstruction with the goal of property-aware lead optimisation.

Why molecular properties matter

A closer look at the drug-discovery context behind LeadOptAI’s molecular-property learning.

The six descriptors used in LeadOptAI
Molecular weightLogPRotatable bondsTopological polar surface area (TPSA)Hydrogen-bond donorsHydrogen-bond acceptors

These physicochemical descriptors characterise molecular size, lipophilicity, flexibility, polarity, and hydrogen-bonding capacity. LeadOptAI learns molecular properties and uses property values as context for SMILES infilling. The descriptors are relevant to drug-discovery decisions, but they do not replace experimental ADMET assessment.
